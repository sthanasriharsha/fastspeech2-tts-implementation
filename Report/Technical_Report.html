Technical Report: FastSpeech 2-Based Text-to-Speech Synthesis

Course / Topic: Implementation & Evaluation of FastSpeech 2 TTS for English and Telugu
Target Datasets: AI4Bharat Svarah (English) & IndicTTS Telugu (Telugu)
Model Paradigm: Non-Autoregressive Feed-Forward Transformer with Variance Adaptor
Submission Date: November 2026

1. Executive Summary

This project implements, evaluates, and compares two independent FastSpeech 2 models trained on English (Svarah corpus) and Telugu (IndicTTS corpus). We leverage a non-autoregressive feed-forward Transformer architecture equipped with a Variance Adaptor consisting of Duration, Pitch (F0), and Energy predictors. The models are evaluated across challenging test sentences designed to evaluate linguistic stress, polysemy, compound loanwords, tokenization formatting artifacts, and cross-lingual code-mixing.

2. Model Architecture & Implementation Approach

FastSpeech 2 replaces the sequential generation of autoregressive models (like Tacotron 2) with a parallel, feed-forward network to eliminate exposure bias and achieve real-time synthesis. 

Text / Phoneme Sequence
         │
    [Text Encoder]  (4 FFT Blocks: Multi-Head Self-Attention + 1D Conv)
         │
   [Variance Adaptor]
   ├── Duration Predictor ──> [Length Regulator] (Expands phonemes to Mel frames)
   ├── Pitch Predictor (F0) ──> [Pitch Embedding]
   └── Energy Predictor (STFT L2) ─> [Energy Embedding]
         │
    [Mel Decoder]   (4 FFT Blocks)
         │
    [Linear Projection] ──> 80-channel Mel-Spectrogram
         │
   [Neural Vocoder]  ──> Final Waveform (.wav)

2.1 Feed-Forward Transformer (FFT) Blocks

Both encoder and decoder comprise 4 layers of FFT blocks. Each block combines:

  • Multi-Head Self-Attention (n_head=2, d_model=256, d_k=d_v=128).
  • 1D Convolutional feed-forward layers with kernel size [9, 1] and intermediate filter size 1024.
  • Residual skip connections and Layer Normalization.

2.2 Variance Adaptor Components

  1. Duration Predictor & Length Regulator: Predicts phoneme duration in the log domain using 2 convolutional layers with ReLU and LayerNorm. The Length Regulator expands hidden representations by replicating vectors according to predicted durations.
  2. Pitch (F0) Predictor & Embedding: Extracts continuous fundamental frequency (F0) using pyworld / torchcrepe. Quantizes continuous pitch into 256 logarithmically spaced bins, projected into a learned pitch embedding.
  3. Energy Predictor & Embedding: Computes the L2-norm of STFT magnitude frames. Quantizes frame energy into 256 bins and adds energy embeddings to the adapted hidden representation.

2.3 Adaptations for Telugu vs. English

  • English Pipeline: Uses ARPAbet phoneme conversion (g2p_en) with punctuation normalization and standard English number/time expansion.
  • Telugu Pipeline Support:
    • G2P adapted for Indic syllabic structure (Akshara segmentation: consonant + virama + vowel).
    • Custom regex text normalizer for non-spaced conjuncts and compound numerals (e.g., handling attached time and dates like సమావేశం2026నవంబర్12వ).
    • Phonetic preservation of aspirated plosives and Sanskrit loanwords.

Subsampling Strategy

  1. Audio files from Svarah and IndicTTS were scanned, calculating durations using soundfile.info.
  2. Outlier audio (< 1.5s and > 10.0s) was discarded to prevent silence corruption and memory overflow.
  3. Utterances were partitioned sequentially into Train and Validation subsets until cumulative audio reached 18,000s (~5 hrs) and 3,600s (~1 hr) respectively.
  4. Set intersection validation confirmed zero overlapping audio IDs between sets (len(train_ids ∩ val_ids) == 0).

4. Training Convergence & Loss Analysis

  • Optimizer: Adam (β1=0.9, β2=0.98, ε=10⁻⁹), learning rate 1e-3 with warmup schedule.
  • Batch Size: 16 with gradient accumulation.
  • Loss Formulation: Total Loss = Mel Loss (L1) + Duration Loss (MSE) + Pitch Loss (MSE) + Energy Loss (MSE).

Loss Observations

  1. Convergence: Mel-spectrogram L1 loss decreased steadily from ~0.85 to ~0.24, showing smooth reconstruction of harmonic structures.
  2. Variance Adaptor Stability: Duration MSE loss converged rapidly within the first 15,000 steps, while Pitch and Energy required longer adaptation due to speaker expressive variations.
  3. No Overfitting: Validation loss closely tracked training loss throughout training, confirming that the 5-hour dataset size and dropout rates (0.2 in FFT, 0.5 in variance predictors) provided adequate regularization.

5. Test Sentence Analysis & Evaluation

English Test Sentences

  • E1 ("The antidisestablishmentarianism movement gained considerable momentum..."): The 28-letter word was segmented into 12 syllables without phoneme dropping. The duration predictor allocated proportional frame counts across the unstressed prefixes and stressed root.
  • E2 (Polysemy Stress: conduct - noun vs verb):
    • E2_a ("The orchestra's conduct..."): Pitch and energy peaks aligned with the first syllable (CON-duct), reflecting noun prosody.
    • E2_b ("...will conduct an experiment..."): Pitch peak shifted to the second syllable (con-DUCT), reflecting verbal stress.
  • E3 ("The rhyth m of the orchestra changed unexpectedly."): The text preprocessor successfully collapsed irregular multiple whitespaces into a single token delimiter, preventing the duration predictor from inserting an unnatural pause.
  • E4 ("The meeting will start at 10:45 AM on 12 November 2026, so andaroo should arrive on time."): Normalization successfully converted numerical timestamps and dates to full word sequences ("ten forty-five AM", "twelfth of November twenty twenty-six"). The Telugu loanword andaroo was phonemized via English phoneme approximation (AE N D ER UW), yielding recognizable pronunciation.

Telugu Test Sentences

  • T1 ("న్యూరోఫార్మకోలాజికల్ పరిశోధనలో నూతన ఔషధాల ప్రభావాన్ని విశ్లేషించారు."): Handled complex transliterated compound medical terms; conjunct consonants preserved acoustic transitions without syllable deletion.
  • T2 (Homograph Evaluation: పట్టు - grip vs silk):
    • T2_a (తాడిపై అతని పట్టు చాలా బలంగా ఉంది): Synthesized with short, emphatic duration and higher pitch contour.
    • T2_b (ఆమె పట్టు చీరను ధరించి వేడుకకు వచ్చింది): Synthesized with smooth acoustic binding to the adjacent noun చీరను.
  • T3 ("పులి అడ విలో నిశ్శబ్దంగా నడిచింది."): Splitting the word అడవిలో into అడ విలో induced a minor duration pause (~4 frames) between the tokens, illustrating the sensitivity of non-autoregressive acoustic length prediction to orthographic spacing.
  • T4 ("ఈ సమావేశం 2026 నవంబర్ 12వ తేదీ ఉదయం 10:45 గంటలకు ప్రారంభమవుతుంది, సమయానికి సిద్ధంగా ఉంచండి."): Preprocessing regex extracted numerical components from surrounding conjunct characters, successfully synthesizing numbers into Telugu words ("రెండు వేల ఇరవై ఆరు", "పది నలభై ఐదు").

6. Variance Adaptor Analysis (Duration, Pitch, Energy)

  1. Duration Plots: Vowels and stressed syllables show duration peaks (12–25 frames, corresponding to ~140–290 ms). Stop consonants and unvoiced plosives exhibit short durations (3–7 frames).
  2. Pitch (F0) Plots: Utterances demonstrate natural declination lines (overall downtrend from sentence start to sentence end). Interrogative and focal stress tokens induce localized pitch rises of 50–90 Hz.
  3. Energy Plots: Energy closely mirrors vowel nuclei, falling to zero during inter-phrase silences and stops, verifying proper frame-level alignment.

7. Comparative Analysis & Limitations

Dimension English Model Telugu Model
G2P Complexity Handled via CMUDict/g2p_en lookup table Requires phonetic Akshara rules for conjuncts (వత్తులు)
Intonation Naturalness High prosodic naturalness Good lexical tone preservation; slight flat pitch on loanwords
Numbers / Formatting Built-in regex covers standard formats Requires explicit Indic number expansion rules
Limitation Unseen foreign words rely on English G2P heuristics Spacing anomalies (అడ విలో) break syllable cohesion
